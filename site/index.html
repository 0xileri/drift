01The insight

Attention and money move at different speeds.

A token can get loud before real volume shows up — an early signal, or a paid pump. It can also move serious volume while almost nobody is talking, which is what quiet accumulation looks like from outside.

Neither number means much alone. A social score is a popularity contest; a volume chart is a crowd you cannot see the face of. The information is in the disagreement, and that gap is the only thing this agent reports.

03The engine

Three corrections, each found by measuring

Momentum is a log ratio

Percent change is unbounded as the previous value nears zero. On a thin pool, one quiet hour followed by an active one produced scores in the six figures — division by almost nothing, not momentum. And thin pools are what this agent is built to watch.

before  max |z| 178,497
↓ log ratio
after   max |z| 6.2 Bounded — but not cured.

A floor, relative to each pool

A ratio between two tiny numbers is still noise wearing a plausible score, and 62% of the first events had an hour under $1,000. The floor scales to each pool's own median, because a flat cut would have deleted 68% of the thinnest token's hours and 3% of the deepest.

The two axes were not comparable

Subtracting two z-scores assumes they mean the same thing. Social chatter is a dense count that moves smoothly; pool volume is spiky and often near zero, so its own spikes inflate the yardstick it is measured against.

↓ percentile rank
now p99  social 0.995  onchain 0.978 Each side is now its own percentile, uniform by construction, so the two match at every quantile rather than at the one a scale was tuned to.

Visible in the labels: the social-to-onchain split moved from 3.78:1 to 0.96:1. The lopsidedness was a property of the metric, not of Base tokens.

“Followed” means the lagging series averaged at least 25% above its own pre-event baseline afterwards. 131 events, threshold 1.7, 12 tokens. Regenerate with npm run scorecard.

The agent used to publish readings and never check them. Every explanation ended with "watch the next few intervals" and nobody watched. The hourly series was already on disk, so the test cost nothing — it had simply never been run.

The claim tested is the narrow one this agent implies: after the two series come apart, the lagging one moves. Measured on levels rather than scores, because a rank falls after a spike by construction and comparing scores would report a fade for almost any event.

Read it plainly: flagged hours do worse than hours picked at random, and the gap does not close at any threshold tested. On this month of data, the score does not identify hours where money is more likely to follow attention.

The engine still measures what it says it measures. What is not established is that the measurement leads anywhere — and publishing that is the point. A claim that cannot fail is not a claim.

The likeliest defect is on the input side: attention is measured globally while volume is measured in one Base pool, so the two describe different markets. Scoping both to the same one is the next thing to try.

05Verification

Every figure traces to a real call

Market data arrives through an aggregator — fast and reliable, but still somebody's summary of the chain. So when an event fires, the agent goes to the source: one direct getReserves() call reads the pool's live reserves and compares them against the reported liquidity.

That check is deliberately the same move as the product itself — catching two sources disagreeing — applied one layer deeper. Pools that expose it get it; concentrated-liquidity and Uniswap V4 pools do not, and the agent reports that rather than pretending otherwise.

Output is committed to a public file on every run, so the record is timestamped and append-only. You can check what the agent said and when.

08Operation

Running continuously

A scheduled job polls every hour, rebuilding its baseline from the provider APIs rather than trusting cached state — a stale cache could only ever feed the engine an older picture than the source. A delayed or missed run costs nothing and repairs itself on the next pass.

Readings are keyed to the hourly bucket they describe, not the clock time they were taken, so repeated runs cannot double-count an hour. Narration is deduplicated the same way.
